## 3. Crop Pathology & Agronomic Coverage

### 3.1 Sugarcane (*Saccharum officinarum*)
- **Target Diseases:**
  - **Red Rot (*Colletotrichum falcatum*):** Diagnostic pith reddening with transverse white chalky bands and alcoholic fermentation odour.
  - **Sugarcane Rust (*Puccinia melanocephala*):** Powdery reddish-brown uredinial pustules on foliar lamina during cool, humid periods (November–February).
  - **Sugarcane Smut (*Sporisorium scitamineum*):** Diagnostic whip-like curved black apical sorus; secondary spread via airborne chlamydospores.
  - **Sugarcane Grassy Shoot (*Candidatus* Phytoplasma):** Stunted tillers, profuse chlorotic grassy shoots, loss of apical dominance.
- **Agronomic Management:**
  - **Sett Treatment Protocol:** Moist hot air treatment (54°C at 95% RH for 2.5 hours) followed by fungicidal dip in Carbendazim 50% WP @ 1.0 g/L for 15 minutes.
  - **Fertigation Scheduling:** Recommended Adsali ratio of 400:170:170 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare split across formative, tillering, and grand growth phases via drip irrigation.
  - **Trash Mulching:** Alternate furrow mulching with 8–10 tonnes/ha cane trash to conserve 25–30% soil moisture and suppress weeds.

### 3.2 Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Target Diseases:**
  - **Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*):** Concentric "target-board" rings on older leaves with chlorotic yellow halo and fruit stem-end rot.
  - **Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*):** Rapidly expanding water-soaked greenish-black lesions with cottony white sporulation under cool, fog/dew conditions (12–22°C, RH > 90%).
  - **Bacterial Spot (*Xanthomonas vesicatoria*):** Small dark circular spots with prominent yellow halos on foliage; scab-like fruit blisters.
  - **Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV):** Upward cupping, dwarfing, and floral abortion transmitted by Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*).
- **Agronomic Management:**
  - **Raised Bed & Mulching:** 120 cm x 15 cm raised beds with 30-micron silver-black plastic mulch to prevent soil splashing.
  - **Staking & Pruning:** Bamboo trellising and removal of bottom 30 cm foliage to promote canopy ventilation.
  - **Chemical Formulations (CIBRC):** Preventative Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L; curative Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 1.0 ml/L or Difenoconazole 25% EC @ 0.5 ml/L (PHI: 3–5 days).

### 3.3 Onion (*Allium cepa*)
- **Target Diseases:**
  - **Purple Blotch (*Alternaria porri*):** Sunken elliptical lesions with distinct concentric reddish-purple centers and bright yellow margins.
  - **Stemphylium Leaf Blight (*Stemphylium vesicarium*):** Straw-coloured spindle-shaped lesions turning dark olive-brown with dense soot-like conidial coverage.
  - **Iris Yellow Spot Virus (IYSV - Tospovirus):** Diamond-shaped straw-coloured eye-spots on flower scapes and leaves; transmitted solely by Onion Thrips (*Thrips tabaci*).
- **Agronomic Management:**
  - **Broad Bed Furrows (BBF):** 120 cm bed top width with 15 cm deep furrows to prevent root asphyxiation in heavy Vertisols.
  - **Foliar Wetting Agent (Sticker):** Mandatory addition of non-ionic surfactant (@ 0.5 ml/L) due to the thick waxy leaf cuticle of alliums.
  - **Nutrition:** Application of 20–25 kg/ha elemental sulphur and 50 kg/ha K2O to thicken bulb scales and prevent storage rots.
  - **Thrips IPM:** Blue sticky traps (@ 25–30 traps/ha), border cropping with maize/sorghum, and rotating Fipronil 5% SC (@ 1.5 ml/L) and Spinetoram 11.7% SC (@ 0.8 ml/L).

## 4. General Maharashtra Agronomy & Weather-Risk Advisory
- **Vertisol Drainage:** High-clay black cotton soils require shallow drainage furrows at 15–20 m intervals to discharge cloudburst storm runoff.
- **Disease Outbreak Thresholds:**
  - Relative Humidity > 80% for > 6 hours triggers rapid fungal spore germination.
  - Prolonged free leaf surface moisture (> 4 hours) accelerates *Phytophthora* and *Alternaria* colonization.
- **Spray Safety Constraints:**
  - **Wind Speed:** Do not spray when wind exceeds 15 km/h to prevent spray drift.
  - **Rain Risk:** Delay spray if rain is predicted within 4–6 hours to avoid wash-off.
  - **Temperature:** Avoid peak heat (> 32°C) between 12:00 PM and 3:30 PM to avoid phytotoxicity.
